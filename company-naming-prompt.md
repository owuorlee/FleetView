# Prompt: Name our Kenyan vehicle-tracking company

Copy everything below this line into a fresh AI agent session.

---

You are a brand strategist and naming expert with deep knowledge of the Kenyan market, Swahili and Kenyan vernacular languages, and East African consumer culture. Your task is to develop and rigorously evaluate name candidates for a new company, and recommend one. Think carefully and show your reasoning at each stage — the screening matters more than the brainstorm.

## The company

A GPS tracking platform for cars and motorbikes in Kenya. Two faces, one brand:

1. **Consumer:** individual car owners and boda boda riders. Anti-theft peace of mind, a lightweight bilingual (English/Swahili) Android app, remote engine cut-off, and M-Pesa-native renewals with transparent pricing. Bought after theft scares; recommended rider-to-rider at boda stages; sold through SACCOs and car-electronics installers.
2. **B2B:** asset financiers and SACCOs that finance hundreds of thousands of motorbikes and require trackers as collateral protection — portfolio dashboards, payment-linked immobilization, recovery tooling. Corporate buyers who need the brand to sound credible in a boardroom and on a loan contract.

Positioning: *"The tracker that renews itself and answers to your loan book"* — trustworthy to a rider in Kisumu, credible to a credit manager in Nairobi. Current working placeholder: **FleetView** (evaluate it honestly as a baseline candidate; do not anchor on it).

## Hard requirements — every candidate must pass all of these

1. **Radio/stage test:** easy to say and hear in both English and Swahili — no consonant clusters that mangle in either language; unambiguous spelling when heard once ("spell it over a bad phone line").
2. **Scope test:** must not exclude cars, trucks, or fleets (not bike-only), and must not exclude consumers (not finance-jargon-only).
3. **SMS sender ID test:** a recognizable form must fit in **11 alphanumeric characters, no spaces** (alerts and receipts arrive by SMS — the sender ID *is* the brand for many customers). State the sender ID form for each candidate.
4. **Meaning screen:** no negative, comic, or vulgar meaning in Swahili, Sheng, Kikuyu, Dholuo, Luhya, Kamba, or Kalenjin. Flag anything you are unsure about for native-speaker verification.
5. **Distance test:** clearly distinct from Safaricom/M-Pesa trademarks and from existing Kenyan tracking companies (verify against the market as best you can; at minimum check the obvious incumbents you know). Also not confusable with major global brands.
6. **Practical availability:** plausible `.co.ke` and `.com` (or clean modifier like `get-`/`-app`) domain; company name registrable at BRS Kenya. If you have web access, check; if not, mark each candidate "needs check".

## Soft criteria (weight in scoring)

- **Trust & permanence** (30 %): sounds like a company you'd let cut your engine and hold your location data. No gimmicks.
- **Memorability at the boda stage** (25 %): one hearing, sticks. Short (ideally ≤ 3 syllables).
- **Bilingual warmth** (20 %): feels Kenyan-owned, not a foreign white-label; bonus if it carries meaning in Swahili without being a cliché (avoid overused words like *safari*, *simba*, *jambo*, *twiga*).
- **B2B credibility** (15 %): works on a financier contract and an insurer partnership deck.
- **Growth headroom** (10 %): won't embarrass us if we add fuel sensors, insurance telematics, or expand to Tanzania/Uganda (check meaning there too).

## Process — follow in order

1. **Territories:** brainstorm across at least these five naming territories, ~8 candidates each (≈ 40 total): (a) Swahili/vernacular words or blends (protection, sight, path, home-safe...); (b) coined/invented words with Bantu-friendly phonetics; (c) English descriptive/compound; (d) metaphors (guardian animals, landmarks, navigation stars — Kenyan resonance, not colonial-safari kitsch); (e) hybrid EN/SW blends.
2. **Kill round:** apply the six hard requirements; show what died and why (one line each).
3. **Score:** rate survivors (aim 8–12) on the weighted soft criteria in a table; be a harsh grader.
4. **Deep dive top 3:** for each — pronunciation notes, story/meaning, SMS sender ID, domain options, tagline in English *and* Swahili, one risk and its mitigation, how it looks on a boda sticker and on a financier dashboard login page.
5. **Recommend one**, with a two-sentence rationale, and name the runner-up.
6. **Verification list:** end with the human checks required before committing (BRS name search, KIPI trademark search, native-speaker meaning review per language, domain purchase, social-handle sweep).

Do not present a name as "available" unless you actually verified it; distinguish clearly between *checked* and *needs check*.
